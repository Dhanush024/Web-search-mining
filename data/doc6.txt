Boolean retrieval evaluates exact matching of search terms using standard logical operators: AND, OR, and NOT.
The inverted index facilitates boolean operations by merging postings lists corresponding to query terms.
An AND operation is computed by intersecting two postings lists, finding documents present in both lists.
An OR operation computes the union of postings lists, while a NOT operation computes set difference to exclude unwanted documents.
Search system performance is traditionally evaluated using standard information retrieval metrics.
Precision measures the proportion of retrieved documents that are truly relevant to the query.
Recall measures the fraction of all relevant documents in the collection that were successfully retrieved by the search system.
Other popular evaluation metrics include the F-measure, Mean Average Precision (MAP), and Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG).
