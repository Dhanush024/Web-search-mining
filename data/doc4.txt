Tokenization and text normalization are foundational preprocessing steps in natural language processing and search engines.
Tokenization breaks a raw sequence of characters into individual terms or tokens, stripping away unwanted punctuation and whitespace.
Following tokenization, text normalization converts tokens into standardized canonical forms.
Case folding is a primary normalization step that transforms uppercase letters to lowercase, ensuring that Search and search match the same dictionary entry.
Other normalization techniques include accent stripping, contraction expansion, and morphological stemming.
Stemming algorithms, such as the famous Porter Stemmer, strip grammatical suffixes from words like retrieval, retrieved, and retrieving to reduce them to their common stem.
Stop words filtering removes frequent grammatical words such as the, is, at, which, and on, which contribute little semantic value to general search queries.
